Dermoscopy & Models

Dermoscopy reveals sub-surface pigment structures invisible to the naked eye. This instrument runs those close-up images through a deep-learning model whose probability output was calibrated, and whose referral threshold was frozen, on a held-out split it never trained on — the same discipline a lab applies to any measurement instrument before trusting its readout.

What does this number mean?

The probability shown is the model's calibrated estimate that the image resembles melanoma, learned from patterns in training data — it is not a diagnosis, and involves no physical exam or patient history.

The referral threshold isn't a 50/50 guess — it's deliberately set low, so the model flags more images for review. That trades lower specificity for higher sensitivity: catching more true melanomas at the cost of more false alarms. The model's own error rates are shown with every reading, so the number is never seen without its limits.

Only two models are offered here because only two have a frozen, calibrated operating point. A third architecture trained in this project, EfficientNet-B3, reaches similar raw accuracy but has no deploy.json — its calibration would need the original HAM10000 validation split, which this demo doesn't have access to. Rather than show an uncalibrated number next to two calibrated ones, it's left out of the comparison entirely.
